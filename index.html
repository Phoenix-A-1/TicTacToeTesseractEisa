Tic Tac Toe Tesseract

4D nested tic-tac-toe: 729 cells, 81 E-grids, 9 G-grids

Made by Ahmad Eisa Siddique

How to Play & Coordinate Notation ▼

Goal of the game

Get three connecting tiles in a line: vertically, diagonally or horizontally. Three cells in a line win a middle tile (E), three middle tiles in a line win an outer tile (G), and three outer tiles in a line win the whole Tesseract. The marks are always X and O.

The board (729 squares)

  • Outer squares (outer lines): 9 tiles marked α (alpha) to ι (iota), written Gn.
  • Middle squares (middle lines): 9 tiles inside each outer tile, marked A to I, written En.
  • Inner squares (cells): 9 cells inside each middle tile, numbered 1 to 9.
  • Every square corresponds to a number. Outer: α = 1 … ι = 9. Middle: A = 1 … I = 9. Inner: 1 to 9. So 3 = γ = C, 8 = θ = H, and so on.
  • 9 × 9 × 9 = 729 squares in total.

The small corner number on the outer and middle squares is the number each one corresponds to.

Writing coordinates

A move is written as Gn En (x/o) n. The commas aren’t needed when you write the actual letters. Example: βBx8 means outer β, middle B, an X on inner square 8.

Where you send your opponent

Let n be the number of the inner square you just played. It decides where your opponent must play next. m is the move number (it only matters when you record a game). The first move of the game can go anywhere.

Method 1: the longest way

n sends your opponent to Gn, and they may play on any middle tile and any square inside it.

Example: Player 1 plays βBx8. Player 2 must play inside θ (the 8th Greek letter), on any middle tile.

Insane version: n sends them to Gn and En, so the move after βBx8 must be on tile H of θ.

Method 2: blocks of 3

For 3 moves, players play a standard game of Ultimate Tic Tac Toe inside one outer tile (n picks the middle tile). The n of every 3rd move picks the new outer tile for the next block, so the player who starts each block alternates.

Normal: βBx3, βCo1, βAx4, δAo9 (any middle tile on δ).

Insane: βBx3, βCo1, βAx4, δDo7 (must be middle tile D).

Method 3: the shortest way

Finish a whole Ultimate Tic Tac Toe game inside one outer tile (n picks the middle tile each move). When that outer tile is decided, the n of the final move picks the next outer tile.

Normal and insane both apply at the handoff.

Insane variant, in short: Movem+1 = n → Gn → En. Normal: n → Gn → anywhere in Gn.

Decided tiles (the "free move" rule)

  • A tile is decided when it has been won or drawn (a full tile with no line counts as a draw and belongs to nobody).
  • If you are sent to a decided E, you may play on any other E in that same G. (The "Free move" setting can widen this to anywhere on the board.)
  • If you are sent to a decided G, you may always play on any other G.
  • So sending your opponent to a finished tile hands them a free move. It’s usually a mistake.

Using this app

  • Yellow cells are where you may play. The last move has a dark outline.
  • Focus G zooms into one outer tile (great on phones). It follows the tile you were sent to, and you can tap the α–ι buttons to look around.
  • Resign and Offer draw work against the AI and online. The AI accepts a draw only if it does not think it is better. Online, your opponent gets a popup to accept or decline.
  • Pause game (online) asks your opponent to stop for now. The game stays open for 48 hours and you can both resume it from your Profile while online. If nobody resumes it in time it ends in a draw.
  • Thinking time (Hard AI) lets you choose how long the bot searches per move, from 0.5 to 20 seconds. The clock above the board counts total game time, and it appears in the exported log with the winner, loser, difficulty and game mode.
  • Analysis tab: the Hard bot reviews every move after (or during) a game, scoring accuracy for the middle game and endgame (the opening is skipped unless you include it), lists best and weak moves, and exports the report.
  • Accounts: Guests can play online natively! Creating an account gives you a persistent profile with game stats, friends list, and game invites.
  • Online: create a room and share the 6-letter code or invite a friend directly, then chat. The host controls the rules. If the opponent disconnects you can claim the win. Undo is off online.
